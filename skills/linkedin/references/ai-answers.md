# LinkedIn in AI answers

Whether LinkedIn posts and articles help a brand get named or cited in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers. Checked October 2026. Measuring it per engine belongs to [ai-search](../../ai-search/SKILL.md).

## What the evidence says

- **A top source, but not on every engine.** LinkedIn was the third most-cited domain across engines in Peec AI's March 2026 data (30M sources), mostly for professional and B2B questions. Profound (680M citations, August 2024 to June 2025) put it fourth in Google's AI Overviews (1.3% of citations) and fifth on Perplexity, and outside ChatGPT's top ten. SE Ranking (100K keywords, October 2026) found social links in 68% of AI Overview answers and 32% of AI Mode answers but only 8% of ChatGPT's, with LinkedIn under 1% of ChatGPT answers. Plan on Google's surfaces and Perplexity first.
- **People, not pages.** 75% of LinkedIn citations point to members' content, 25% to company pages (Meltwater with LinkedIn, 9.5M citations, August 2026; sponsored, method not published).
- **Articles over posts.** LinkedIn's own guide (August 2026) says long-form articles earn more AI citations than feed posts and that freshness counts. The most-cited LinkedIn articles were how-tos, how-to-choose guides, comparisons, ranked lists and buyer explainers; all used lists, 92% had clear subheads, 67% carried statistics (Meltwater). Opinion pieces alone did less well.
- **A rented channel.** An engine can stop citing LinkedIn the way ChatGPT stopped showing Reddit threads in August 2026. Use LinkedIn articles to put the brand into the sources engines read, and keep the canonical version of anything important on the user's own site.

## What to do with it

- When the user's AI search baseline shows LinkedIn among the cited sources for their buyer questions, the founder and team writing articles that answer those questions directly (the question in the title, a list, a number, a date) is cheap, owned work.
- Prefer people's articles and newsletters to company-page posts for this job.
- Re-measure on the same prompts after two to four weeks with [ai-search](../../ai-search/SKILL.md); no study has measured how long a LinkedIn article takes to be cited.
- Van der Blom calls LinkedIn the second most-quoted source in AI tools (June 2026); he gives no method, so prefer the figures above.
