# Local ranking factors

What decides who shows in the local pack and on Maps, what decides local organic results, and what is folklore. Checked October 2026. No one outside Google measures these weights directly: the best sources are Google's own short description, a large expert survey, and small controlled tests. Say which kind of evidence a claim rests on.

## What Google says

[Google's help page](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) names three things: **relevance** (how well the profile matches the search; a complete profile helps), **distance** (from the location in the search, or from where Google thinks the searcher is), and **prominence** (how well known the business is, including links, articles and the number and score of reviews). It says a farther business can outrank a nearer one when it is judged a better match, that replying to reviews helps, and that there is no way to request or pay for a better local rank. It gives no weights.

## The 2026 survey

Whitespark's Local Search Ranking Factors (published 6 November 2025; 47 practitioners scoring 187 factors; a survey of opinion, not a measurement) is the standard reference. Its top five for the **local pack and Maps**:

1. Primary Business Profile category
2. Proximity of the address to the point of search
3. Keywords in the business name
4. A physical address in the city searched
5. The business being open at the time of search (new in 2026)

For **local organic** results: a dedicated page for each service, city or neighbourhood relevance of the content, quality of links to the domain, keywords in the title of the page the profile links to, and links from sites in the same industry.

Moves since the 2023 edition that change advice: predefined services on the profile rose from 81st to 22nd, keywords in services from 110th to 35th, and proper hours from 85th to 21st; internal links from the profile's landing page fell from 45th to 110th. Citations kept losing weight for rankings, while the survey's new AI column rates them higher for AI answers.

**Negative factors** at the top: marked permanently closed, wrong primary category, another profile in the same category at the same address (the filter), 1 and 2 star ratings, marked temporarily closed, a misplaced pin, a hacked site, and no hours.

**Suspension risks** at the top: a PO box, mail store or virtual office address; a profile tied to a suspended Google account or to other suspended profiles; reports of violations; a hacked site; review gating; fake reviews; keyword stuffing in the name. Too many reviews too fast ranks 12th.

## Proximity

- **It is the factor the business controls least, and it moves the pack most.** In our test three Columbus ZIP codes a few kilometres apart showed 8 different practices in their 9 pack slots, and suburbs 8 to 30 km from the centre shared none with the city's pack ([field notes](field-notes.md)). Local Falcon, which sells grid tracking, says even a small move changes results.
- **Maps weighs distance harder than the search page's pack.** For "dentist" around downtown Columbus, Maps at the centre put the three nearest practices first, while the pack on the search page, at the same city and ZIP, showed three practices 2 to 4 km out with 839 to 1,118 reviews each; the two lists shared none (October 2026, [field notes](field-notes.md#the-maps-tools-live-16-october-2026)). Prominence seems to carry further on the search page (two locations, one search), so the pack can show a business that Maps ranks top three in only part of town.
- **Distance is measured from the searcher, or from the place named in the query.** "Dentist" searched from Columbus and "dentist columbus ohio" searched from elsewhere in the US shared one of three pack places in our test. Treat "service" from inside the town and "service in town" as different searches.
- **A business cannot rank everywhere in a city from one address.** Expect a strong ring around the address that fades with distance; how fast it fades depends on how many competitors sit closer to each searcher. A grid shows where the ring ends ([rank grid](rank-grid.md)).
- **Moving the pin or listing a nearer address to win a district is the most common route to suspension** (false addresses top the survey's suspension risks). The honest levers for a far district are a real second location, a page for that district on the site, and prominence (reviews, links, mentions) that outweighs distance.

## Relevance: category, services, name

- **Primary category first.** It is the top factor in the survey and a wrong one is the second worst negative. Choose the category that names what most customers search for, not the broadest one; add secondary categories only for services actually offered. Changing categories can trigger re-verification ([Business Profile](business-profile.md#verification-and-re-verification)).
- **Services and their descriptions now count.** Fill the predefined services Google offers for the category, then add custom ones in the customer's words.
- **Keywords in the business name work, which is why spammers use them.** Google's guidelines require the real-world name as on signage and documents. A name with keywords added is a suspension risk and a legitimate target for a spam report (see [competitors](competitors.md#spam-in-the-pack)). A business that genuinely trades under a descriptive name has an edge that is fair to keep.
- **The website still matters for the pack.** The page the profile links to, its title and its city relevance feed both the pack and local organic results. For a multi-location business, link each profile to its own location page, not the homepage.

## Prominence: reviews, links, mentions

- Google names review count and score. Practitioner studies (correlational, mostly vendor) find count and recency associate with pack position more than average rating; the rating mostly decides whether the searcher clicks ([reviews](reviews.md)).
- Links and local press feed both prominence and the organic results; [link-building](../../link-building/SKILL.md) and [digital-pr](../../digital-pr/SKILL.md) own that work.
- Being named on local best-of lists is the survey's top factor for AI answers, and AI engines cite such lists for "best X in town" prompts ([AI answers](ai-answers.md)).

## Folklore to drop

- **Citation building as the main lever.** Consistent name, address and phone across the big directories still matters for trust and for AI engines that read Yelp and similar sites, but mass-submitting to hundreds of directories is not where rankings come from.
- **Service areas as a ranking tool.** Rankings follow the verified address; adding towns to the service area does not make a business rank there ([Business Profile](business-profile.md#service-area-businesses)).
- **Posting weekly to the profile to rank.** No study shows posts move rankings. Posts can help a searcher who already found the profile.
- **Geotagging photos.** Sterling Sky's test on five profiles (January 2024) found no lasting effect, Google often strips photo metadata on upload, and John Mueller has said there is no need to geotag images for SEO.
- **Click-through manipulation** (paid clicks or bots on the listing). Behavioural signals feature in the survey, but faked ones are spam and short-lived.
