# Channel benchmarks

Numbers users ask for ("what's a good CAC", "is 3% engagement good") with the method behind each, so the agent quotes them with the right weight. Most channel benchmarks are vendor data from their own customers; few disclose a sample size. Use them for orders of magnitude, then replace them with the user's own numbers within 90 days. Checked October 2026.

## Organic social engagement

Two large datasets disagree by an order of magnitude because they divide by different things. Never set one vendor's rate against another's.

| Platform | Buffer median, 2025 (engagements ÷ reach) | Socialinsider average, 2025 (interactions ÷ followers) |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | about 6.1% (6.4% in 2024) | not covered |
| Instagram | about 5.4% (7.3%) | 0.48% (0.50%) |
| Facebook | about 5.6% (5.0%) | 0.15% (0.15%) |
| X | about 2.8% (2.0%) | 0.12% (0.15%) |
| TikTok | about 4.5% (4.4%) | 3.70% (2.50%) |
| Threads | about 3.6% (4.4%) | not covered |

Buffer: 52M+ posts by Buffer users, 2024 to 2025, accounts with 10+ posts; Buffer warns that a change can reflect who posts rather than the platform. Socialinsider: about 70M brand posts, January 2024 to December 2025; its page has internal inconsistencies (TikTok 3.70% or 3.73%), and Facebook brands halved their posting from 47 to 24 posts a month.

Use these to sanity-check an account, not to grade it: compare an account with its own median over time, and a post with the median of the account's recent posts.

## What not to quote

- Visitors or signups "per Product Hunt launch": consultant estimates without method ([launch](launch.md#what-a-launch-yields-now)).
- "AI traffic converts 4x to 23x better": built on single sites.
- Any CAC by channel without the number of companies behind it.
- Organic reach percentages ("Facebook 1-2%", "Instagram 3.5%") from secondary blogs: the original measurements could not be traced.
