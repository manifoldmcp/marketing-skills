# Sourcing services

Services where journalists post questions and experts answer: HARO and its successors. What happened to them, what is running in October 2026, and how to use them without wasting the user's time.

## What happened

- **HARO** (Help a Reporter Out) ran from 2008; Cision owned it from 2014 and renamed it **Connectively** in 2024 with paid tiers. Cision shut Connectively down on 9 December 2024.
- **Featured.com bought HARO from Cision** (announced 16 April 2025) and relaunched it as a free, newsletter-sponsored daily email of journalist queries, free for journalists and sources. Featured runs its own paid expert-answer platform beside it (Featured had over 50,000 experts and 2,500+ outlets in its network at the acquisition).
- **Source of Sources** (SOS), started by HARO's founder Peter Shankman in 2024 after Cision's changes: free, email-based, a few queries a day.
- **Qwoted**: a free tier with limited pitches and paid plans (Pro about $99 a month in early 2025), with a searchable request board and expert profiles.
- Others: Help a B2B Writer (free, B2B), SourceBottle (Australia and New Zealand), ResponseSource (UK, paid), ProfNet (Cision).
- What Featured has done with the HARO and Connectively names since mid-2026 is reported differently by different blogs. Check helpareporter.com directly before telling a user how it works or what it costs.

## What to expect

- **No trustworthy link-rate data exists.** Blogs quote follow-link rates per platform (one claims 36% for SOS) and approval rates (one claims about 6% on Qwoted and 18% to 20% on Featured), but none publishes a method or sample we could check. Do not quote them as benchmarks.
- **Competition is heavy and much of it is AI-written.** Every general query draws a pile of generic answers; journalists and the platforms filter for them (Featured's relaunch named human expertise as its point). An answer that reads like a chatbot is deleted unread.
- **Links are not guaranteed.** Many outlets that use sourcing services quote without linking, or link only to a bio. Count mentions, not just links (see [measurement](measurement.md)).
- **The outlets vary from national to content farms.** Some queries come from content sites gathering free quotes for SEO listicles. Check the outlet before answering: is it a real publication, does it rate above the floors in [link-building](../../link-building/SKILL.md), would the user's buyers read it?

## How to use them well

- **Answer few, answer fast, answer specifically.** Queries close within hours or a day. Pick the ones squarely in the spokesperson's expertise; a specific, first-hand answer to one query beats ten generic answers.
- **The answer is the quote.** Two to four sentences the reporter can paste, with a number or an example only the user has, then the name, title, company and one link. No sales copy.
- **Set the routine, not the volume.** Fifteen minutes a day reading the digests for the spokesperson's topics is the usual commitment. Track queries answered, placements and links in a sheet; after a month, stop answering the query types that never land.
- **Never answer on the user's behalf or under their name without them.** Draft answers for the spokesperson to send.
