# Pitching journalists

What reporters say they want from a pitch, from the two large 2026 surveys, and what that means for the email the user sends. Checked October 2026.

## The two surveys

- **Muck Rack, State of Journalism 2026** (published 19 March 2026; 897 journalists in the release, surveyed 30 January to 2 March 2026; Muck Rack's research page says nearly 1,100). Mostly US. The full report sits behind a form; some figures below come from Muck Rack's own summary and from several secondary write-ups that agree with each other.
- **Cision, State of the Media 2026** (published 13 May 2026; 1,899 journalists in 19 markets across North America, EMEA and APAC, surveyed January and February 2026). Vendor survey; the release gives headline numbers only.

Both companies sell PR software. Treat them as the best available evidence, not as neutral science, and say so when quoting them to a client.

## What they say

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| At least some stories began with a PR pitch | 86% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Use pitches as the start of more than half their stories | about 5% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Use PR material (releases, pitches, kits) for story ideas | 66% | Cision 2026 |
| Delete pitches outside their beat at once | 88% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Say a pitch relevant to their beat is rare | 43% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Say fewer than a quarter of pitches are relevant | 72% | Cision 2026 |
| A pitch is relevant when it affects their audience or community, not just their topic | 78% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Prefer a one-to-one email | 62% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Prefer pitches under 200 words | 69% | Muck Rack 2026 |
| Seldom or never reply to pitches | about half | Muck Rack 2026 |

## What it means for the email

- **One reporter, one story, one reason.** The opening line names what the reporter wrote and why this story extends it for their readers. "Relevant" to a journalist means it matters to their audience, not that it shares a keyword with their beat.
- **Under 200 words.** The story in the subject line, the news or the number in the first sentence, what the reporter gets (data, a spokesperson, images, an embargo), and nothing about the product's features. Attach nothing large; link to the data, method and press kit.
- **Lead with what they rank highest.** A named, available spokesperson with a real view; original data with its method and sample; images they can use. A bare announcement with a quote from the CEO is what they delete.
- **One follow-up**, three to five working days later, adding something (a new number, a local angle, a customer who will talk), not "bumping this". A Cision figure compiled by BuzzStream puts "follow up once" at 64% of journalists and "never" at 8% (edition not stated). Repeated chasing is one of the reasons journalists give for blocking a sender.
- **Timing.** No current survey measures day of week. The only time figure is Muck Rack's 2024 survey: 44% prefer pitches before noon in their own time zone. Reactive pitches go out the same morning the news breaks, or not at all.
- **Email first.** LinkedIn matters to journalists as a platform (Cision) but no survey shows they want pitches there. A connection request or a comment on their post is a fine second touch, never a pitch by direct message to a stranger.
- **AI drafts.** Over half of journalists say they oppose AI-written pitches, and several say they can tell. If the user drafts with AI, every fact, figure and reference to the reporter's work must be checked by a person, and the result must read like the user wrote it. Never send a batch of near-identical AI pitches; that is the "spray and pray" pattern that earns the fewest links (see [stories](stories.md#benchmarks)).

## Exclusives and embargoes

- **An embargo** is a promise not to publish before a set time. Ask whether the reporter accepts the embargo before sending the details; send them only after the yes, with the lift time and time zone in the subject line. A reporter who breaks one comes off the list. Cision lists embargoed information among what journalists value most.
- **An exclusive** gives one outlet the story first. It gets that outlet's full attention and can cost coverage elsewhere. Offer it only when that outlet reaches the user's buyers better than the others combined, and say clearly when the exclusive ends.

## Honesty rules

- Never invent a statistic, a customer, a quote or a reporter's past article. Journalists fact-check, and one invented claim ends the relationship.
- Every number in a pitch says where it came from (the user's data, a named public source, a Manifold pull with its date).
- Never pay for coverage that is presented as editorial. Paid placement must be labelled as sponsored by the publisher.
- The user sends. Write pitches only when asked, one per reporter, tied to that reporter's article.
