# Rates and rights

What creators are paid, which deal fits which goal, and what the user is buying beyond the post: usage rights, ads from the creator's handle, exclusivity. Checked October 2026. Prices move fast and vary by niche and country; quote every figure with its source and date.

## What creators are paid

- **Marketplace transactions, 2025 (Collabstr 2026 report).** Average paid collaboration about $186 on TikTok, $193 on Instagram (listed $214 before negotiation) and $255 on YouTube; nearly 80% of collaborations under $300. Campaign share: Instagram 40%, UGC 35%, TikTok 21%, YouTube 3%. TikTok-only campaigns fell 48% and UGC campaigns rose 133%. These are completed deals on one marketplace of mostly small creators, taken from Collabstr's own summary (the full report could not be fetched for this check). They say what a small creator charges, not what a mid-tier or agency-managed creator charges.
- **Market size.** US creator ad spend was $29.5B in 2024, forecast at $37B for 2025 (up 26%) and $44B for 2026, growing about four times faster than media overall (IAB, November 2025). Budgets are rising: brands are competing for the same mid-tier creators.
- **Self-reported intent, not spend.** In Influencer Marketing Hub's 2026 survey (600+ respondents, small per-question bases), about half planned to expand nano and micro work, against about a fifth for macro. Treat it as mood, not data.

## Who to pay

- **Smaller creators return more per dollar.** Low-follower targeting returned an ROI an order of magnitude higher than high-follower targeting across three metrics, because engagement falls as followership grows (Beichert, Bayerl, Goldenberg and Lanz, Journal of Marketing, 2024: 1,881,533 purchases and three field studies with hundreds of paid endorsements, direct-to-consumer seeding).
- **But engagement peaks in the middle.** Across 802 Instagram campaigns with over 1,700 influencers, engagement with sponsored posts rose with follower count and then fell (an inverted U); content customised to the creator's audience and less familiar brands flattened the fall (Wies, Bleier and Edeling, Journal of Marketing, 2023).
- **What that means:** for sales, many small and mid creators beat one large one for the same money; for reach in one moment, size buys certainty of delivery. Twenty gifted nano creators teach more in a month than one paid macro post: pay only after the gifting shows which creators and angles move people.

## A fair price

- **Effective CPM** is the fee divided by one post's expected views, times 1,000, where expected views is the creator's organic median, never followers. Compare it with the user's paid-social CPM on Meta or TikTok. At several times that, the post must bring something ads cannot (trust, a demo the user can reuse as an ad, a search-ranked YouTube review that keeps earning views) or the price comes down.
- **Manifold's working rule of thumb for the US** (not a measured benchmark): flat fees around $15 to $35 per 1,000 median views for a TikTok or a Reel and $20 to $50 for a YouTube integration, about double for a dedicated video. YouTube reviews that rank in search keep earning views for months, so their lifetime CPM is lower than the first month's.
- **Price the add-ons separately, before the creator quotes:** paid usage of the post (Spark Ads, partnership ads, the user's own ads) is commonly priced at 20% to 50% of the fee per 30 days, category exclusivity at 10% to 30%, raw footage and extra hooks per item. These are practitioner norms from rate cards, not a published dataset.

## Deal types by goal

| Goal | Deal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Learn what works, cheaply | Gifting or seeding to 15 to 30 nano and micro creators | Most gifted creators post; none have to. The ones who post and sell are the paid shortlist |
| Sales the user can count | Affiliate code or link per creator, or a small fee plus commission | A code per creator is the only clean attribution, and it still undercounts (viewers forget codes and buy later) |
| Reach on a date | Flat fee with a go-live date in the contract | Only a paid, agreed post is a commitment |
| Content to run as ads | UGC per video with usage rights ([UGC](ugc.md)) | The user pays for the craft and the rights, not the creator's audience |
| Ads that look organic | The creator's own post run from their handle (Spark Ads, partnership ads) | Rights and a duration are part of the price |

Re-hire the creators whose sponsored posts beat their own median. A sponsored post at half the creator's median is a warning, whatever the follower count.

## Running posts from the creator's handle

- **TikTok Spark Ads:** the brand runs a creator's organic post as an ad, with the creator's authorization code; the authorization duration is set when the code is made. A sparked video can run up to 10 minutes, and a post must be un-authorized before the creator can delete it (TikTok Ads help, updated June 2026). TikTok publishes no lift figures on that page; the [paid-ads](../../paid-ads/SKILL.md) skill owns the ad side.
- **Meta partnership ads** (formerly branded content ads): run from both the creator's and the brand's handles with the creator's permission, given in the app or through a partnership ad code the creator shares (Instagram Help, up to two partners per code). The post needs the paid partnership label.
- **YouTube:** creator videos with paid promotion declared can be run as ads through Google Ads where the creator links the channel; check the current setup in Google Ads before promising it.
- **State in the brief, before the quote:** which posts, which platforms, from whose handle, for how many days, and whether edits are allowed. Rights the brief does not name, the user does not have.

## Affiliate commissions

- **Amazon Associates (US standard rates, fetched October 2026):** 10% luxury beauty; 5% music, digital video and handmade; 4.5% kitchen, automotive and physical books; 4% apparel, shoes, jewellery, watches, luggage, Amazon devices and all other categories; 3% toys, furniture, home, garden, pets, beauty, sports, outdoors, tools and baby; 2.5% PCs and components; 2% TVs; 1% grocery, health and personal care, and video games. A creator choosing between the user's program and Amazon compares against these.
- **Other programs:** commission is set by each brand; read the competitor's own affiliate page (the host can open it) and match or beat it on the categories that matter. No primary dataset on SaaS or TikTok Shop commission norms was found for this check: say so rather than quoting a "typical" rate.
- **Coupon, cashback and deal sites** claim credit for sales that would have happened anyway; leave them out unless the user wants them.

## What the tools cannot see

Fees, contracts, codes redeemed and sales. They see public posts and numbers, and a paid boost only when it shows in an ad library. Ask the user for code redemptions and sales at each checkpoint.
