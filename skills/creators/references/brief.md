# The creator brief

What the user sends each creator before they post: what to make, what to say and not say, how to disclose, and what the user may do with it afterwards. Good briefs give the message and the evidence and leave the words to the creator. Never send it; hand it to the user.

## Ground it in the niche

About 12 credits:

- **Sponsored posts that got watched:** `tiktok_search_videos` with the niche keyword and `sort: "popular"`, `instagram_search_posts` with the niche hashtag, two pages each; keep rows with a sponsored signal, and add competitors' names as queries.
- **How they open and sell:** `tiktok_get_transcript` or `instagram_get_transcript` on the five most-watched: the hook, when the product comes in, story, list or demo, and the ask. The hooks that work organically in the niche are the reference for creator posts; ad hooks fit UGC the user will run as ads (see [paid-ads](../../paid-ads/SKILL.md)). Say which this brief is.
- **What the audience asks:** comments on three of those posts. The brief asks creators to answer the top three questions or objections on camera.

## What it says

1. **The brand in two lines,** the goal and the KPI (sales through a code, sign-ups through a link, awareness, content to reuse).
2. **One message and at most three talking points.** A creator asked to land five features lands none.
3. **Deliverables:** platform, format, length, count, draft due, live date. Default one TikTok or Reel of 30 to 60 seconds, one draft round, live within three weeks.
4. **Hooks that work in the niche,** each with an example link; the audience's questions to answer.
5. **Do:** your own words and hook, the product in use, one clear call to action with the code. **Don't:** read a script, claim what the user cannot prove (health outcomes, earnings, "best"), name or mock competitors, before-and-after images where the platform bans them, a price that may change.
6. **Disclosure,** as a condition of payment ([disclosure](disclosure.md#what-the-brief-must-say)).
7. **Usage rights and exclusivity:** which uses, whose handle, how many days, and the Spark Ads code or partnership-ads permission asked for by name. Rights and exclusivity cost money; state them before the creator quotes ([rates](rates.md#running-posts-from-the-creators-handle)).
8. **Approval:** one draft, one round of changes, and what the user checks (claims and disclosure, not the creator's style).
9. **Tracking:** the creator's own code and link. Without them the user cannot tell which creator sold.
10. **Fee and payment terms,** left for the user to fill.

## Judgment

- Brief the message, not the script. Scripted posts sound like ads and fall below the creator's own median, the number the user will judge them by.
- A UGC brief covers videos for the user's channels (rights, raw files, extra hooks); an influencer brief covers posts on the creator's own (disclosure, go-live date, comments).
- For a launch, add the embargo and the dates in [strategy](strategy.md#creators-for-a-launch); an embargo is a request, not a contract, so send nothing that cannot leak.
- The tools read public posts: they cannot see a post's paid boost, saves on Instagram or sales. The tracking line is how the user will measure it.
