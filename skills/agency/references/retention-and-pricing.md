# Retention and pricing

Why clients leave, how long they stay, and what AI changed about agency pricing and scope, from the 2025-2026 surveys. Almost every source here is a survey of agencies or a vendor's; the method is given with each so it can be weighed. Checked October 2026.

## Why clients leave

| Reason | Clients say (Setup 2025) | Agencies say (Setup 2025) | Agencies say (AgencyAnalytics 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dissatisfied with delivery | 61% | 18% | 31% (performance below expectations) |
| Dissatisfied with value | 61% | not in the top list | 32% (lack of perceived value over time) |
| Agency did not understand the business | 44% | 3% | |
| Dissatisfied with the relationship or strategy | 41% each | 15% and 8% | 9% (communication or trust) |
| Budget cuts | 39% | 75% | 42% |
| Client leadership change | 35% | 55% | 37% (client-side internal changes) |

Sources: Setup with the CMO Institute, 2025 Marketing Relationship Survey, about 100 responses (57% clients, 43% agencies; client respondents include large brands), published January 2026. AgencyAnalytics 2026 Marketing Agency Benchmarks, 494 agency staff (64% US, half at agencies of 1 to 10 people), February to April 2026; the reasons are what agencies believe.

- **The gap is the finding.** Clients' value complaint rose from 36% to 61% in a year, and agencies still blame budget. When a client gives budget as the reason, ask what value they expected and did not see; the honest answer is often there.
- **Small businesses leave fast and over return.** 40% of SMBs that outsource marketing have dropped or switched an agency; 56% of those exits came 6 to 12 months in; 60% gave lack of return (vcita, 500 US SMBs in home services, health, beauty and business services, April 2025; vendor survey).
- **Clients would mostly stay.** 72% of clients were not likely to switch in the next six months, 83% would hire their agency again, and the average score was 7 of 10 (Setup 2025). Churn is a minority acting on unmet value, not a mass mood.

## How long clients stay

The figures disagree because the populations do:

- **Enterprise agency-of-record**: about 7 years on average, more than double the 2016 figure (4As and ANA, April 2025; method not public). Secondary write-ups give 8.1 years without mandatory reviews against 3.8 with frequent reviews.
- **US agencies serving mostly small and mid-sized clients**: 57% say a typical client lasts 12 to 24 months, 3% expect three years or more (CallRail, 100 US agencies, 2026; CallRail sells call tracking and links short lifespans to agencies that do not report ROI, a correlation from its own survey).
- **Agency self-report across sizes**: 62% say the average client stays two years or more (AgencyAnalytics 2026).
- **Annual retention** of 78% for seven-figure and 92% for eight-figure agencies (Predictable Profits, 300+ agencies, 2025; secondary, method not seen).

Use the 12 to 24 month figure for a small-client retainer business. "PPC churns 49% a year" comes from one agency's own book (Focus Digital, 2026) and is not a benchmark.

## What keeps them

- Agencies rank strong relationships (80%) and communication (69%) above campaign performance (39%), transparent reporting (32%) and clear ROI (30%) as retention drivers (AgencyAnalytics 2026). Read with the client data above: relationship and communication are how value is shown, not a substitute for it.
- Clients engaged on three or more services: 56% (AgencyAnalytics 2026). More services usually means a stickier client, but no study separates cause from effect.
- Most new clients still come from referrals (85% of agencies) and word of mouth (72%); outbound 33%; AI search 11% (AgencyAnalytics 2026). A retained client is also the main source of the next one.

## Pricing and scope after AI

- **Fixed fees are rising.** A quarter of agencies had moved entirely to fixed-fee pricing, and 63% of marketers using fixed fees were satisfied; more than half of the rest were interested (Forrester Consulting for Dentsu Creative, 356 US and Canadian marketing and procurement leaders, May 2026). Dentsu Creative sells fixed-fee work, so read it as a direction, not a share. About two-thirds of respondents worried that agency pricing is not connected to outcomes.
- **AI has not crashed prices.** 81% of CRO agencies raised or held rates in 2026 and 15% cut them; of those that raised, 72% did it by moving to higher-value work; no measure of AI use predicted price direction (Convert, 55 self-selected CRO agencies, February to May 2026). The feared wave of AI discount requests had not arrived, and only 3% reported significant AI-driven staff cuts (Productive, 174 of its own agency customers, March 2026).
- **Most agencies keep AI as a margin tool.** 61% of US agencies treat AI as a cost of doing business and 6% sell it as a line of business; 31% plan to sell AI agents within two years (Forrester with the 4As, about 200 VP-level agency leaders, April 2026; the report is not public). Forrester's reading: agencies are harvesting AI productivity for margin rather than growth.
- **Scope is where the pressure lands.** 44% of agencies say clients expect faster turnaround, 30% that clients see the work as cheaper, 25% less tolerance for manual work (AgencyAnalytics 2026), and agencies report clients asking for AI search reporting and strategy at no extra charge. 46% of agencies name project scoping as a top challenge (Setup 2025).
- **Retainers still dominate.** The 2025 prediction that agencies would shift to project work did not happen among CRO agencies (Convert 2026). Clients are also taking work in-house: 69% of CRO agencies say clients are bringing testing in-house.

**What follows for pricing:** price the outcome and the judgement, not the hours AI now saves; write AI search measurement and the AI work itself into the scope as named line items rather than letting them creep in free; and when moving to a fixed fee, define the deliverables and the change-request rule in the contract, because scope, not rate, is where a fixed fee loses money.

## Unknown

No independent study measures client churn from the client side at scale (Setup's 2025 sample is about 100), whether fixed fees improve retention, or whether reporting AI visibility keeps clients longer.
