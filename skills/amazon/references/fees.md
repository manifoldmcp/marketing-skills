# Fees and unit economics

What selling on Amazon costs in 2026 and how to judge whether a product pays. Fee tables change at least once a year and differ by category, size tier and marketplace: quote the user's numbers from Amazon's Revenue Calculator or the Fee Preview report, never from memory. Checked October 2026.

## What changed in 2026

- **FBA fees:** up an average of $0.08 a unit from 15 January 2026, under 0.5% of an average item's price; Amazon said there would be no new FBA fee types in 2026 and gave at least 90 days' notice of increases (Amazon, 15 October 2025). The detail is in Seller Central help page G201411300. Sellers' guides report standard-size items priced $10 to $50 rising more than cheaper ones and a larger low-price FBA discount for items under $10; the guides disagree on exact amounts, so check the rate card.
- **Fuel and logistics surcharge:** 3.5% of the FBA fulfilment fee from 17 April 2026 in the US and Canada and for Remote Fulfillment from the US; Buy with Prime and Multi-Channel Fulfillment from 2 May 2026. Amazon put the US average at $0.17 a unit and called it temporary, with no end condition; its 2022 surcharge took over a year to unwind.
- **Referral fees:** the category percentages were largely unchanged for 2026. Most categories take 8% to 15% of the total sale price; check the category's row.
- **The fees that surprise sellers** are not the headline ones: inbound placement fees when shipments are not split across Amazon's warehouses, low-inventory-level fees on fast sellers kept too thin, monthly and aged storage, returns processing on high-return products, and inbound defect fees for shipment problems.

## Does it pay?

Per unit, from the user's numbers:

- price, minus referral fee, minus FBA fulfilment fee and surcharge, minus storage per unit, minus landed cost (product, freight, duty), minus returns allowance = contribution before ads.
- Ad cost per unit is total ad spend over total units (TACoS times price), not ACoS times price, because organic sales carry part of it.
- Break-even ACoS is the contribution margin before ads, as a percentage of price. A launch can run above it for a set period on purpose; a mature product should not.

Price points matter because fees are partly flat: a $12 item can lose a third or more of its price to fees before product cost, while a $40 item in the same size tier loses far less proportionally. Check the low-price FBA rates for items under $10.

## Money back

- **Brand Referral Bonus:** a credit against referral fees on sales Amazon attributes to the brand's own off-Amazon traffic through Amazon Attribution links, about 10% of the sale on average, varying by category, counted for 14 days after the click; brand owners only. Commonly quoted category rates range from 7% to 30% but sources disagree; check the user's Brand Referral Bonus page. It lowers the cost of sending email, social and creator traffic to Amazon. No Amazon source says the traffic also lifts organic rank.
- **New-seller incentives** (credits on early branded sales, inbound shipping and Vine for new Brand Registry sellers) change often; check what the account is offered.

## What Manifold can and cannot show

Rivals' prices (`amazon_search_products`, `amazon_get_product`, `amazon_get_sellers`) and whether they are FBA (`ships_from`). Not fees, costs, sales or margins: those need the user's own account. Never estimate a rival's profit from its price.
