# Business types

The context file has the same sections for every business, but each section asks a different question depending on what the business sells and to whom. Settle the type first, from the site or the user's first answer, then read the sections through it. A business that is two types (a SaaS company with a services arm, a shop with a wholesale line) picks the one that brings most of the revenue and notes the other in the product overview.

## B2B software and services

- **Buyer**: a company. Name the team that uses it, the role that signs, and the company size and industry that fit best.
- **Personas**: usually two or three: the user, the buyer, and the person who can block the purchase (IT, finance, legal).
- **Conversion**: a signup, a trial, a demo booked or a sales call.
- **Proof**: customer logos, case studies with a number, review site ratings, security and compliance badges.
- **Competitors**: named vendors, plus the spreadsheet, the in-house build, the agency or doing nothing.
- **Usual first goals**: leads and outbound, search traffic, AI answers, competitors.

## Ecommerce and consumer brands

- **Buyer**: a person. Describe them by need, occasion and budget rather than by job title; age and location only where they change what sells.
- **Personas**: often the buyer and the person it is for (a gift, a child, a pet), when they differ.
- **Conversion**: a purchase, first order value, a repeat purchase or a subscription.
- **Proof**: reviews and star ratings, units sold, press mentions, creator and customer content.
- **Competitors**: other brands in the same product type, the marketplace listings (Amazon, Etsy) and the cheaper generic option.
- **Usual first goals**: social growth, creators, paid ads, search traffic for product terms.
- **Also record**: the price range, the shipping countries, and where it sells besides its own site.

## Local and service businesses

- **Buyer**: a person or a business within reach of the location or the service area. Name the area.
- **Personas**: usually one: the person with the problem, often at the moment they need it (a leak, a booking, a deadline).
- **Conversion**: a call, a booking, a quote request or a visit.
- **Proof**: Google reviews and their count, years in business, licences, before and after photos.
- **Competitors**: the other businesses that show up for the same search in the same area, and doing it yourself.
- **Usual first goals**: search traffic for local terms, reviews and mentions, communities, social for the area.
- **Also record**: the address or service area, the opening hours, the Google Business Profile if there is one.

## Consumer apps and subscriptions

- **Buyer**: a person, by the job the app does for them and the moment they reach for it.
- **Personas**: the core user and the casual user, since they convert and churn differently.
- **Conversion**: an install, an account, the first key action, a paid upgrade.
- **Proof**: app store rating and review count, active users, press and creator coverage.
- **Competitors**: other apps in the same store category, and the habit or tool the user has now.
- **Usual first goals**: social growth, creators, AI answers, communities.
- **Also record**: the app store links and the platforms it runs on.

## Creators, media and education

- **Buyer**: the audience, by topic and level (beginner, advanced), and the sponsors or customers who pay if they differ from the audience.
- **Personas**: the reader or viewer, the paying member or student, the sponsor.
- **Conversion**: a subscriber, a follower, a course sale, a sponsor booked.
- **Proof**: audience size, open or view rates, student results, past sponsors.
- **Competitors**: the other creators and publications on the same topic, and the free alternative (YouTube, a book).
- **Usual first goals**: social growth on the main platform, content ideas, search traffic, links and press.

## Agencies and consultancies

- **Buyer**: the client companies, by size, industry and the problem they hire for. The context file describes the agency itself; a client's details belong in the client's own file, in the client's project.
- **Personas**: the client's decision maker and the day-to-day contact.
- **Conversion**: a discovery call, a proposal accepted, a retainer signed.
- **Proof**: client results with numbers, case studies, client logos, referrals.
- **Competitors**: other agencies and freelancers in the same niche, the in-house hire, the software that replaces the service.
- **Usual first goals**: agency work (pitches, onboarding, reports), leads and outbound, positioning.

## Marketplaces and platforms

- **Buyer**: two sides. Write the target audience, personas, pain points and objections for both sides, marked supply and demand, and say which side is harder to win now.
- **Conversion**: one for each side (a listing created, a first booking).
- **Proof**: transactions or listings, ratings on both sides, coverage in each city or category.
- **Competitors**: the other marketplaces, and the offline or direct way the two sides meet now.
- **Usual first goals**: the side that is short: search and communities for demand, outbound or creators for supply.

## Nonprofits and causes

- **Buyer**: the donors, volunteers or members, and separately the people the work serves.
- **Personas**: the small donor, the large donor or funder, the volunteer.
- **Conversion**: a donation, a recurring gift, a volunteer signup, an event attendance.
- **Proof**: outcomes with numbers, the share of money spent on the work, ratings from charity evaluators, press.
- **Competitors**: other causes competing for the same donors' attention, rather than rivals.
- **Usual first goals**: search traffic, social, links and press, communities.
