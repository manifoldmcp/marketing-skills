# Ad briefs

A creative brief tells whoever makes the user's ads (a designer, a video editor, UGC creators, the founder with a phone) what to make and why. It rests on three kinds of evidence: the ads the category keeps paying for, the words customers use about the problem, and the hooks that hold attention in the niche. It ends in angles to test, not finished ads. Write full copy or scripts only when the user asks.

## What changed: diversity over variants

- On Meta, retrieval matches each ad to the people its content suits (Andromeda, see [platforms](platforms.md#meta-the-creative-is-the-targeting)), so the creative set is the targeting. A brief of one concept in ten crops gives the system one ad; three distinct concepts give it three audiences to find.
- So the brief's unit is the **concept**: a different person, problem, setting, proof or format. Variants (hooks, headlines, colours) come after a concept has won.
- On TikTok and Reels the same holds by a different route: the first seconds decide distribution, and native-looking posts beat produced spots.
- LinkedIn and Google search ads lead with the headline: a job title or a search term. Do not force a video hook there.

## Evidence, best first

1. **The user's own ads with results.** Their best ad beats any competitor's. Ask for it, and its numbers, first.
2. **Customer words.** The best hooks are a customer's sentence, not a marketer's: reviews, support tickets, sales calls, Reddit threads. Finding and mining them belongs to [voice-of-customer](../../voice-of-customer/SKILL.md).
3. **Proven ads in the category.** The long-runners from [competitor ads](competitor-ads.md), read as in [reading an ad](ad-libraries.md#reading-an-ad): the hook, the angle, the proof (a number, a review, a demo, a before and after), the offer, the CTA, and what happens in the first three seconds as far as the transcript shows it.
4. **Organic hooks in the niche.** What holds attention on TikTok or Reels carries over to ads there: [tiktok](../../tiktok/SKILL.md), [instagram](../../instagram/SKILL.md).

Without a swipe file in hand, a small sweep costs about 24 credits: two keywords at three list pages, ten ads read, eight transcripts.

## Three angles

- **One proven:** used by two or more long-runners, so it is table stakes.
- **One from customer words** that no competitor ad uses.
- **One against the main competitor's weak spot,** from the objections customers raise.

For each: the insight in one line with the quote behind it, three hooks, the one message of the body, the proof to show, the CTA, and two or three concepts that carry it.

## The brief

- Objective, and the metric the user will judge it on in their ad account (cost per result, CTR, the share of viewers past the first three seconds). The evidence shows what survives, not what converts; say so.
- Audience and awareness: unaware of the problem, aware of the problem, comparing solutions, or aware of the product. Default: aware of the problem.
- The offer and the landing page.
- The three angles, as above.
- Format and specs per placement: length, aspect ratio (9:16 for Reels, Stories and TikTok; 1:1 or 4:5 for feeds), captions on, product or problem in the first three seconds.
- References: library links of the ads each angle draws on.
- Do and don't.
- A test grid (angle x concept x format) with the number of ads to make, sized to what the team can produce in a week.

## Don't list

- Claims the user cannot prove ("best", "number one", guaranteed results).
- Before and after images in health and weight loss.
- On Meta, any line that asserts the viewer's personal traits ("Are you in debt?"). Meta has also limited US health and wellness advertisers' optimisation for lower-funnel conversion events since early 2025 (as widely reported then); check the account's restrictions before briefing a conversion campaign there.
- Another brand's footage, claims or lines. Take the structure only.
- Creator content used as an ad needs usage rights in writing; that brief belongs to [creators](../../creators/SKILL.md).

## Judgment

- Three angles with three hooks each teach more than one polished ad. Test the angle first, then polish the winner.
- Under retrieval that matches creative to people, ad fatigue on Meta most likely means the concept set is too narrow rather than the audience used up (an inference from how Andromeda works, not a measured finding). Try a new concept before a new colour.
- Ask for results before the next brief. A brief that cannot learn from the last one repeats it.
