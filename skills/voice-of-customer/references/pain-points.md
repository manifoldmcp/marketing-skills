# Pain points

The problems buyers name, clustered into jobs and ranked, with quotes as proof. Pain points are the problem; [objections](objections.md) are doubts about a solution. Ends in one ranked table.

## Collect

- **Search the problem, not the product.** Phrasings people use when stuck: "struggling with <job>", "how do you <job>", "<job> is killing me", "hate <task>", "is there a better way to <job>", "frustrated with <category>". Product names find reviews and vendors; problem words find people with the problem.
- **Read replies, not just titles.** People describe a pain in replies ("same here, we lost a client over this") more than in titles. On Reddit, comment search reaches them across threads; elsewhere read the comments of the busiest posts.
- **Window:** the past year by default. Older pains may be solved, and the tools people name change.
- **Take raw rows** from every source: the quote, its link, the author, the date and the score or likes. Synthesis needs the author to dedupe and the link to prove.
- **Filter sellers first** (see [sources](sources.md#sellers-in-the-sample)). A builder's validation post states the problem in the builder's words, not the buyer's.

## Cluster

- Group by the underlying problem, not the words: "takes forever to load" and "so slow on my phone" are one pain. Name each cluster in the buyers' words. Split a cluster that hides two jobs.
- Write each as a job: "When <situation>, I want <outcome>, but <what gets in the way>".
- A feature request is a pain stated as a solution: record the problem behind it ("I wish it had X" becomes the job X would do).
- Note the workaround and the cost they name (hours, money, a client, a risk): a spreadsheet, a hack or an assistant is the real competitor.
- Aim for 5 to 12 clusters. Keep the singletons in an "other" list, not the bin: in the oral-care study the needs mentioned least held most of the hidden opportunities.

## Count and rank

- **Distinct authors**, not posts: one person posting five times is one voice. Cap any one thread or video at a third of a cluster's authors, so one viral post cannot carry a pain alone. Twenty agreeing replies in one thread are one thread with strong agreement.
- **Sources** the cluster appears on: a pain on three platforms is more general than one on one.
- **Intensity**, 1 to 3: 1 an annoyance ("a bit clunky"); 2 a cost or a workaround (hours lost, a spreadsheet, a hire); 3 action: switching, paying, cancelling, or asking for a recommendation now ("is there a tool that..." is the strongest intent a public post carries).
- **Score** = distinct authors x intensity; break ties by sources.
- **ICP fit:** where a post shows who is talking (a role, a community made of the ICP), count fitting authors separately. A pain carried mostly by people who would never buy goes below the line, marked.
- **Flag the few who are buying.** A cluster with intensity 3 and few authors can be the best opening.

### Why not rank by mentions

Frequency of mention correlated with measured importance at only 0.21 in the oral-care data (Timoshenko and Hauser, 2019), and a cut at the median count would have lost 29% of high-importance needs and 72% of hidden opportunities (needs customers rate low because nothing serves them yet). Mentions measure what is easy and common to complain about. Use the score to choose what to say first in messaging; use the full list, rare needs included, for product and positioning. A detailed post with numbers ("this costs us six hours a week") outweighs ten one-line agreements.

Likes and upvotes measure agreement and entertainment, not importance or intent. Views measure attention.

## Saturation

Stop collecting when the last five threads or videos read added no new cluster, and say so in the deliverable. That rule finds the common themes, not all of them: in interviews, the equivalent rule held 62% to 79% of all themes ([interviews and surveys](interviews-and-surveys.md#how-many-interviews)). If new clusters still appear at the end of the default sample, say the list is not saturated and offer a second pass with new phrasings and sources rather than more pages of the same search. In our own run three invoice threads each added two or three new themes ([field notes](field-notes.md)).

## Deliver

One table: rank, pain (buyer words), the job behind it, what people do about it now, distinct authors, sources, intensity, score, the competitor named if any, two or three verbatim quotes with links. Under it: the "other" list, the exact phrases people repeat (for [customer language](customer-language.md)), how many threads, posts and comments the table rests on, which sources were not checked, and one line on what each of the top three pains means for the product or the message.

## Honesty

- Write "in 15 threads read", never "most people on Reddit". Counts rank pains against each other; for how many people have the problem, use [market-research](../../market-research/SKILL.md).
- Quote verbatim with the link; never paraphrase inside quote marks, and check every quote against its row before delivering. Leave usernames out.
- Never post, reply or contact anyone; listening on a schedule belongs to [brand-monitoring](../../brand-monitoring/SKILL.md).
