# Validating demand

What settles whether people will pay, once the public signals ([demand signals](demand-signals.md)) have said whether they are interested. The skill designs the test and reads its result; the user runs it. Checked October 2026.

## Why stated interest is not demand

- **People overstate what they would pay.** Across 77 studies, hypothetical willingness to pay exceeded real willingness to pay by about 21% on average (Schmidt and Bijmolt, Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, 2020). The gap is larger for higher-priced and specialty products, and indirect methods such as conjoint overstated more than asking directly, against common belief. An earlier meta-analysis of 28 studies found a median ratio of hypothetical to actual value of 1.35 (Murphy and colleagues, 2005).
- So a survey, an interview "I'd use that", a like or a waitlist sign-up are evidence of interest. Only money is evidence of demand: a preorder, a deposit, a paid pilot, a signed letter of intent with a price.

## The commitment ladder

Rank any test by what it asks the person to give up. Each rung up is stronger evidence and fewer takers.

| Rung | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Click on an ad or a "notify me" button | The message caught attention |
| Email on a waitlist | Mild interest; most never return |
| Email plus qualifying answers (role, company size, current tool, budget) | Interest from the right buyer |
| A booked call | A real problem worth half an hour |
| Card on file, a deposit or a preorder with refund | Willingness to pay |
| A paid pilot or a signed order | Demand |

## Fake doors and smoke tests

- **What they are:** a page, a button or an ad for a product or feature that does not exist yet, measuring who takes the next step. Inside an existing product, a fake-door button tests a feature on real users; for a new market, a landing page with targeted ads tests cold buyers. Use the second for a new product: the user's own audience is warmer than the market.
- **Ad policies block the crude version.** Google Ads disapproves destinations that say they provide no service, under its insufficient original content policy; a bare "coming soon" page fails. Meta requires the landing page to be functional and to reflect accurately what the ad offers. The page must describe a real offer honestly: what it will be, when, what joining the waitlist or preordering means, and a refund for any money taken.
- **Be honest after the click.** Tell the person the product is not ready, what happens next, and keep any promise made. Never take a payment that will not be refunded or fulfilled.
- **Set the bar before the test.** Write the threshold and the sample size down first, or any result will look like success. For reference, the median landing page converted 6.6% of visitors, 3.8% for SaaS pages (Unbounce, 41,000 pages and 57M conversions, 2024): pages with real offers, so a test page should be judged against the user's own baseline or a control, not these.
- **Sample size.** A conversion rate p on n visitors has a 95% margin of about ±1.96 × √(p(1−p)/n): ±1.7 points at 4% on 500 visitors. Telling 2% from 4% between two variants takes about 1,100 visitors each. A test of 200 visitors can only find a large effect.
- **Read the drop between rungs.** Clicks with no completed forms means the promise worked and the offer did not. Open-ended answers on the form often show buyers wanting a different product; read them.
- **One variable per test.** Price, audience or message. Changing two at once leaves the result unexplained.

## Sales conversations

For B2B, ten conversations with buyers who fit the ICP often settle more than a landing page: ask about the last time the problem cost them, what they use now and what it costs, and whether they would pay for a pilot. Interest that does not survive a price is not demand. Buyers' own words from calls, reviews and threads belong to [voice-of-customer](../../voice-of-customer/SKILL.md).

## What to hand back

The test (rung, audience, channel, page or script), the threshold and sample size set in advance, the budget and the length, and how the result changes the decision. Never launch ads, send messages or take payments on the user's behalf.
